Production Fermenters: Large-Scale Bioreactor Systems from 1,000 L to 500,000 L

Scaling a fermentation process from pilot to production changes far more than the size of the vessel. Steam and cooling demand rise sharply, the seed train must deliver a larger and more consistent inoculum, and quality records have to stand up to customer audits or regulatory inspection. A large-scale bioreactor performs only as well as the utilities, controls and supporting equipment designed around it.

KNIK BIO is a fermentation tank manufacturer and bioreactor system supplier based in Shanghai. We design and build production fermenters for industrial and biopharmaceutical fermentation, with project capacities from 1,000 L to 500,000 L. The final volume and configuration of each system are set by the organism, the process and the conditions at your site. Depending on the scope, we supply a single vessel, a set of process modules or a complete production train.

Equipment categories

Production Fermenter Systems for Different Applications

The equipment in this category is offered as three supply options. Two are defined by the application and the level of documentation it demands. The third, process modules, is a delivery format that can be used within either. Many projects combine them, for example a GMP bioreactor system delivered as a set of prefabricated modules.

01

Industrial Fermenters

Industrial fermenters serve microbial production where output and cost per batch drive the investment: enzymes, organic acids, amino acids, yeast, probiotics, feed additives, bio-fertilisers and bio-pesticides. The design priorities are oxygen transfer, heat removal, mixing of viscous broths and short cleaning cycles. The largest vessels in our range belong to this group, where a stainless steel industrial bioreactor of 100,000 L or more is a common requirement.

GMP Bioreactor Systems

GMP bioreactor systems are built for vaccines, recombinant proteins and other products manufactured under Good Manufacturing Practice. The engineering principles match those of industrial equipment, but every design decision must be traceable and documented. Typical specifications include 316L stainless steel for product-contact parts, recorded internal surface finish, material certificates, sterilisation cycles that can be qualified on site, and control software with configurable electronic records and audit trail.

We supply the documentation that supports your Installation Qualification (IQ) and Operational Qualification (OQ). Qualification and process validation remain the manufacturer's responsibility, and our engineers work alongside your validation team through both.

Scope of supply

Complete Production Trains: Beyond the Main Vessel

A commercial-scale fermenter relies on several supporting systems running to the same schedule. Filling a 50,000 L vessel takes inoculum from one or more seed stages, sterile medium prepared on time, and utility capacity sized for the peak, when sterilisation, cooling and aeration overlap. A vessel-only quotation can leave these items out of scope. The gaps then surface during commissioning, when they are slowest and most expensive to close.

Depending on the project, a KNIK BIO production train can include:

  1. Seed train: number of stages and vessel sizes set by the organism, inoculum ratio and transfer timing
  2. Main fermenter: vessel, agitator and mechanical seal, sparger, jacket or internal coils, sampling and harvest valves
  3. Media preparation and sterilisation: batch or continuous, matched to batch volume and media properties
  4. Utility distribution: steam and clean steam, cooling and chilled water, sterile air filtration, exhaust treatment
  5. Cleaning and sterilisation: Clean-in-Place (CIP) and Steam-in-Place (SIP) circuits, shared or dedicated
  6. Automation: PLC/SCADA with alarms, trends and batch reporting; for GMP projects, electronic-record and audit-trail functions can be configured, subject to system validation
  7. Documentation: drawings, P&IDs, material certificates, welding and passivation records, manuals

Where a site already has a boiler house, CIP station or seed equipment, we design the new train around those existing assets.

Process engineering

Engineering Considerations for a Large-Scale Bioreactor

At production scale, vessel volume is rarely the limiting factor. The design effort goes into a handful of areas that decide whether the process performs the way it did in the pilot plant.

01

Oxygen transfer and agitation. Aerobic microbial processes need oxygen delivered evenly throughout the broth. The achievable oxygen transfer rate, usually expressed through kLa, depends on agitator arrangement, impeller diameter, power input, sparger design, airflow, vessel back-pressure and broth viscosity. Dissolved oxygen control then links airflow, agitation speed and pressure to keep the process within its limits as demand changes during the batch.

02

Heat removal. Metabolic heat rises with cell density, and agitation adds more. Because vessel volume grows faster than wall area, a large-scale bioreactor often needs internal coils or extra cooling surface in addition to the jacket. Cooling duty is calculated from the expected heat load of the organism and process, together with the temperature and capacity of the cooling or chilled water available on site.

03

Mixing and broth properties. Filamentous fungi, polysaccharide producers and high-solids media create viscous broths that resist mixing. These processes may need different impeller combinations and higher installed power than an industrial bioreactor running a low-viscosity bacterial fermentation of the same volume.

04

Cleaning, sterilisation and contamination control. Sterility at this scale is designed in from the first drawing: drainable piping, minimal dead legs, suitable valve types, steam access to every sterile boundary, and temperature monitoring at the coldest points during SIP. CIP coverage depends on spray-device selection and placement and is confirmed by testing. Together, these requirements shape nozzle layout, piping slopes and vessel internals.

05

Materials and construction. Product-contact parts are normally 316L stainless steel, with 304 stainless steel used for non-contact structures where suitable. Internal surface finish, weld inspection, material traceability, and design pressure and temperature are specified according to the application and the code requirements of the destination country.

Capacity planning

Working Volume, Vessel Design and Capacity Planning

The capacity of a large-scale bioreactor may be stated as total vessel volume or as working volume, and the difference matters when comparing offers. Working volume is the broth volume the process actually runs at. The remaining space is headspace, which accommodates gas hold-up, foam and, in fed-batch processes, the volume added during feeding. Working volume is often around 70–80% of total volume, depending on aeration rate and foaming behaviour. When comparing proposals, ask each supplier to state both figures.

Annual output can often be achieved with one very large vessel or with several smaller vessels operated on a staggered schedule. Multiple vessels require more capital, but they provide redundancy, smooth out utility demand and allow production to continue while one vessel is cleaned or maintained. The right balance depends on output targets, product value and site constraints, and it should be settled before the building design is fixed.

Plant projects

When Production Fermenters Require Full Plant Planning

Up to roughly 30,000 L, many production fermenters can be fabricated in the factory, shipped largely complete and installed on a prepared foundation. This figure is an approximate planning reference. Vessel diameter and height, the transport route, road and port restrictions, site access and lifting capacity all determine what can travel in one piece.

Above this range, the project usually becomes plant engineering. Vessels may be shipped in sections and completed on site. Steam and cooling demand can call for new boilers or chillers. Foundations, building height, pipe racks and electrical supply must be designed alongside the process. A single coordinated team covering process design, equipment supply, installation and commissioning reduces the risk of scope falling between contractors. Our 100,000 L deliveries to Kazakhstan fall within this category.

Early coordination of vessel dimensions, foundation loads, building height and utility routes prevents costly changes once construction is underway. For projects likely to exceed 30,000 L, we recommend involving the equipment supplier before the building layout is fixed.

Standards & testing

Vessel Standards, FAT, SAT and Documentation

Pressure vessel code. A large-scale bioreactor is a pressure vessel and must comply with the code that applies where it will operate. KNIK BIO designs and fabricates production vessels to the ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code, with ASME stamping available for applicable vessels. The code basis for each vessel is confirmed in the proposal. If another national standard applies, it should be specified at the enquiry stage so that it is included in the design and price.

Factory Acceptance Test (FAT). Before shipment, the system is tested at our factory against an agreed FAT protocol. Typical checks include pressure and leak testing, agitator run-in, instrument and control-loop verification, and sterilisation trials. You or your appointed representative can witness the test and approve the results before the equipment is released for shipping.

Site Acceptance Test (SAT). After installation, key tests are repeated at your site with local utilities connected, followed by water runs and, where specified, sterile hold tests. SAT identifies installation, utility and control issues before the equipment enters routine production.

Documentation. Standard supply typically includes general arrangement drawings, P&IDs, material certificates, welding and passivation records, FAT and SAT reports, operating and maintenance manuals, and recommended spare parts lists. GMP projects also receive surface-finish records and the documentation needed to support IQ and OQ. The full document list is defined in the scope of supply for each project.

Scale-up

Pilot-to-Production Scale-Up

Pilot fermenters are used to develop the process and generate the data needed to design production equipment. A commercial-scale fermenter is designed to repeat that process consistently, batch after batch, at the lowest practical cost per kilogram. The design priorities change with that shift in purpose.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What size counts as a large-scale bioreactor?

There is no formal industry threshold. The term usually refers to production vessels from around 1,000 L working volume upward, as opposed to laboratory and pilot equipment. KNIK BIO's production range covers project capacities from 1,000 L to 500,000 L.

Can you supply only the vessel?

Yes. If your site already has seed vessels, media preparation and utilities, we can supply the industrial bioreactor on its own. In that case, the interfaces with your existing equipment must be clearly defined, and we will review your steam, cooling and air capacity so the vessel's design matches what the site can deliver. Performance of the complete process then depends partly on equipment outside our scope.

Should we choose stainless steel or single-use at production scale?

Single-use bioreactors are widely used in cell culture and are available from some suppliers up to several thousand litres. They reduce cleaning and changeover time but carry ongoing consumable costs, and high oxygen demand in microbial processes places heavy requirements on mixing and heat removal. For high-volume microbial fermentation, stainless steel remains the more common choice. The decision depends on the product, batch frequency, contamination-control strategy and the cost model of the plant.

What utilities does a large-scale fermentation system need?

Typically plant steam, clean steam for sterile boundaries, cooling water and often chilled water, compressed air for aeration and instruments, purified or process water, electrical power and drainage. Peak demand usually occurs when sterilisation and cooling overlap, so utility sizing is based on the production schedule rather than average consumption.

What is the difference between CIP and SIP?

Clean-in-Place (CIP) removes product residue and soil from the vessel and piping using circulated cleaning solutions, without dismantling the equipment. Steam-in-Place (SIP) then sterilises the cleaned system with saturated steam before the next batch. On a commercial-scale fermenter, both are normally automated sequences.

Can production fermenters be customised for different microorganisms?

Yes. Agitation, aeration, cooling surface, vessel geometry and sterilisation design are all set according to the organism and process. A bacterial fermentation, a filamentous fungal process and a yeast process of the same volume can each need a differently configured large-scale bioreactor.

How long does a production fermenter project take?

The schedule depends on vessel volume and quantity, documentation level, material availability, testing requirements and site readiness. Each proposal includes a preliminary schedule showing design, fabrication, FAT, shipping, installation and SAT as separate stages.

Does KNIK BIO provide installation and commissioning support?

Installation, commissioning, SAT and operator training can be included in the scope of supply. The level of on-site support is agreed for each project.
